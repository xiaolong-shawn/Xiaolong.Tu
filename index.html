About

I am Xiaolong Tu (涂小龙), a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Georgia State University, working in the Advanced Mobility & Augmented Intelligence Lab under Dr. Haoxin Wang. My research interests are multimodal foundation models, the efficiency of LLM training and inference, and model benchmarking, compression and neural architecture search.

Most of my work begins with measurement. I build profilers and cost predictors that reveal what a model actually does on real hardware — kernel by kernel, token by token — and then use those measurements to drive architecture search, compression and deployment decisions. My current focus is hybrid attention and state-space multimodal architectures, and understanding what is lost when a large model is compressed into a small one.

Before starting my Ph.D. I spent over ten years as a wireless systems engineer at Ericsson, Huawei, Qualcomm and Apple, working on 3G through 5G system design, interoperability and performance analysis. I received my B.S. from Hubei University in 2012.

Publications

How Do Prompt Variations Affect Energy Consumption in On-Device LLMs?

Wei Hu*, Xiaolong Tu*, Dawei Chen, Kyungtae Han, Onur Altintas, Haoxin Wang

Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP) 2026, main conference

The first large-scale study of how prompt phrasing and cognitive load shape on-device inference energy: 404 prompts, 5 models, 2 phones, 7,620 runs with phase-level profiling. Cognitive load changes energy per token; phrasing changes it mainly through token count.

arXiv/Project

Transferable Latency Prediction for Fast LLM Screening on Heterogeneous Edge Devices

Xiaolong Tu, Vinod K. Mishra, Venkat R. Dasari, Anu G. Bourgeois, Haoxin Wang

ImmerCom 2026, Workshop at ACM MobiCom

A runtime-aware latency predictor that fuses static descriptors with live hardware telemetry, separates prefill from decode, and screens candidate LLMs before expensive on-device execution. Validated on Pixel phones, Jetson Nano, Orange Pi 5 Pro and an RTX 3090-class GPU.

arXiv

Keyword Matters: Unveiling the Energy Sensitivity of On-Device LLM Prompting

Roy Tao, Xiaolong Tu, Haoxin Wang

Edge Intelligence Workshop at the ACM/IEEE Symposium on Edge Computing (SEC) 2026

Real power measurements on a smartphone showing how imperative keywords and instruction structure change decoding length and total energy, making prompt wording a lightweight lever for efficiency.

arXiv

LM-Meter: Unveiling Runtime Inference Latency for On-Device Language Models

Haoxin Wang, Xiaolong Tu, Hongyu Ke, Huirong Chai, Dawei Chen, Kyungtae Han

ACM/IEEE Symposium on Edge Computing (SEC) 2025, Washington, D.C.

Best Paper Award Finalist — top 5%

The first lightweight online latency profiler for on-device LLM inference, costing 2.58% of prefill and 0.99% of decode throughput. It reveals that scaling a model raises prefill latency far faster than decode on phones — the inverse of the datacenter picture — and that three fused GEMM kernels carry over 60% of decode time.

Paper/arXiv/Code

PlatformX: An End-to-End Transferable Platform for Energy-Efficient Neural Architecture Search

Xiaolong Tu, Dawei Chen, Kyungtae Han, Onur Altintas, Haoxin Wang

ACM/IEEE Symposium on Edge Computing (SEC) 2025, Washington, D.C.

A fully automated hardware-aware NAS platform that searches 959,417 architectures in 7 GPU-days, adapts its kernel-level energy predictor to unseen phones from about 100 samples, and finds models reaching 0.94 accuracy or 0.16 mJ per inference.

Paper/arXiv/Code

AIEnergy: An Energy Benchmark for AI-Empowered Mobile and IoT Devices

Xiaolong Tu, Anik Mallik, Haoxin Wang, Jiang Xie

ITU Journal on Future and Evolving Technologies 2025, vol. 6, no. 2

An energy benchmark for AI workloads on mobile and IoT hardware, built from controlled measurement on real devices rather than from proxy metrics such as FLOPs.

Paper

GreenAuto: An Automated Platform for Sustainable AI Model Design on Edge Devices

Xiaolong Tu, Dawei Chen, Kyungtae Han, Onur Altintas, Haoxin Wang

International Workshop on Mobile Computing Systems and Applications (HotMobile) 2025, Palm Springs, CA

An end-to-end platform for exploring, generating, deploying and evaluating sustainable AI models, using Pareto-front search over an expanded NAS space guided by pre-trained kernel-level energy predictors.

Unveiling Energy Efficiency in Deep Learning: Measurement, Prediction, and Scoring across Edge Devices

Xiaolong Tu, Anik Mallik, Dawei Chen, Kyungtae Han, Onur Altintas, Haoxin Wang, Jiang Xie

ACM/IEEE Symposium on Edge Computing (SEC) 2023, Wilmington, DE, pp. 80–93

The first detailed measurement study of on-device deep learning energy across edge hardware, producing kernel-, model- and application-level datasets and the first kernel-level energy predictors for mobile CPU and GPU.
