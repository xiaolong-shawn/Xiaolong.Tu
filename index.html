About

Making a model cheaper is easy. Knowing what you gave up for it is not. Most efficiency work reports an aggregate score and moves on, which hides where the loss actually happened and leaves no way to fix it.

My research tries to close that gap from both ends. On one side I build instruments: fine-grained inference profilers that expose per-stage runtime behaviour across mobile, embedded and GPU platforms, transferable cost predictors calibrated from a handful of measurements, and open benchmarks built from controlled runs on real hardware. On the other I use them to look inside compressed models — tracing where representations and cross-modal alignment diverge between a teacher and its student, and localising downstream degradation to specific components rather than to a number.

Right now that means hybrid attention–state-space multimodal architectures: finding the failure mode, then designing the architectural and training interventions that recover accuracy without giving back the efficiency. Before this I spent ten years as a wireless systems engineer, which is where the habit of trusting measurement over intuition came from.

What I work on

Multimodal models

Efficient hybrid architectures

The efficiency–accuracy trade-off in hybrid attention and state-space multimodal models: locating degradation in the parameter update behaviour of specific components, tracing what fails to transfer during distillation, and designing interventions that recover it.

Efficiency

Training and inference cost

Per-stage profiling of language model inference — prefill against autoregressive decode, throughput, memory pressure — to establish which architectural and serving choices, and which prompts, actually dominate end-to-end cost.

Benchmarking

Compression and model search

Measurement-in-the-loop platforms that combine hardware profiling, learned cost prediction and real-device validation, with transferable kernel-level predictors that generalise to unseen hardware from few-shot calibration.
